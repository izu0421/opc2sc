Analysis code for reproducing the computational figures in the manuscript above — spanning rat in vivo lesion transcriptomics, reanalysis of human multiple sclerosis snRNA‑seq, and two independent in vitro paradigms for inducing OPC‑to‑Schwann‑cell (oSC) differentiation.

1. Preprocessing of OPCs during injury and in controls

Rat, Smart‑seq2. Count matrices (aligned with Cell Ranger) are quality‑controlled, technical covariates regressed out, and cell states annotated.

  1. Dataset preprocessing and removal of low‑quality cells
  2. Regressing out total_counts, pct_counts_mt, pct_counts_ERCC
  3. Cell state annotation
  4. Plotting gene markers

2. Analysis of OPCs during injury and in controls

Differential abundance, trajectory inference and cell fate mapping identify a Schwann‑cell‑primed (SC‑primed) OPC population unique to the lesion (Fig. 1, Extended Data Fig. 1).

  1. Differential abundance analysis (Milo)
  2. Cell trajectory analysis via PAGA
  3. Cell fate mapping via CellRank
  4. Pathway enrichment visualization (STRING)

  5. Transcription factor enrichment visualization (ChEA3)
    extended table ↓
  6. Unbiased marker gene identification (all datasets)

3. Identification of oSCs in human MS lesions

Reanalysis of the published human MS snRNA‑seq dataset (Lerma‑Martin et al., 2024; GSE279183). A CellTypist classifier trained on the rat data is used to transfer labels and identify a discrete population of predicted‑oSC nuclei, restricted to chronic active lesions (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 2).

  2. QC and CellTypist label transfer onto human MS OPC nuclei

4. BMP4/Vitronectin‑treated OPCs

10x Genomics scRNA‑seq of OPCs three days after combined vitronectin and BMP4 treatment, the extrinsic paradigm sufficient to drive OPC‑to‑SC differentiation in vitro (Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 3–5).

  1. Combined QC/preprocessing shared with the SOX10/OLIG2 dataset
  2. BMP4‑specific preprocessing and QC
  3. RNA velocity analysis

5. SOX10 overexpression / Olig2 knockdown (TF manipulation)

Direct genetic manipulation of the SOX10/OLIG2 circuit, sufficient to phenocopy the extrinsic vitronectin/BMP4 paradigm (Extended Data Fig. 6–8).

  1. Preprocessing and QC
  2. RNA velocity analysis
  3. Cell fate mapping via CellRank

6. Comparison of BMP4/Vitronectin‑ and SOX10/OLIG2‑induced oSCs

Joint analysis showing that both induction paradigms converge on the same mature SC‑like state (Extended Data Fig. 8).

  1. Joint trajectory/pseudotime analysis of the combined dataset
  2. Comparison of pseudotime and marker expression between paradigms
  3. Correlation of cell fate driver genes between the two datasets

Running the notebooks

Clone the repository, install the dependencies below, then open notebooks in Jupyter and run in section order. Each notebook is self‑contained given the required input data (raw sequencing data: GEO GSE311740, GSE312112; human MS: GSE279183).

python3.9 – 3.11 (tested on 3.10)
scanpy≥ 1.9.0
anndata≥ 0.9.0
scvelo≥ 0.2.4
cellrank≥ 1.4.0
celltypist≥ 1.6.0
scvi-tools≥ 1.0.0
milopydifferential abundance analysis
cellphonedb≥ 5.0.0
R ≥ 4.2ggplot2 (circle plot visualizations)
